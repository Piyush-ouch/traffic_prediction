Traffic Flow Prediction

Optimizing Intelligent Transportation Systems via Machine Learning Ensembles

1. Aim & Problem Statement

Aim: To study how Machine Learning (ML) techniques can be used in Traffic Flow Prediction to improve Intelligent Transportation Systems (ITS) and drastically reduce urban traffic congestion.

The Problem: Traditional statistical prediction methods and baseline ML models fail to handle rapidly changing urban traffic conditions. Traffic flow is highly non-linear and depends on dynamic spatial-temporal factors:

Weather Time of Day Junctions Day of Week

Existing systems struggle to provide accurate real-time predictions and severely underfit when environmental patterns shift.

2. Methodology & Dataset

Systematic Literature Review (SLR): Research compiled from IEEE, ScienceDirect, and Elsevier analyzing the efficacy of Deep Learning, ANN, and Advanced Tree Ensembles.

Project Dataset Implementation:

  • Source: Urban Traffic Prediction Dataset.
  • Size: 48,122 historical traffic records.
  • Feature Extraction: Raw DateTime strings were mathematically decomposed into engineered numerical cyclical features: Year, Month, Day, Hour.
  • Pre-processing: Anomalies removed, datasets split 80/20, and StandardScaler applied for distance-based ML algorithms.

3. Algorithm Architectures

We implemented and reviewed multiple ML pipelines:

  • Linear/Ridge Regression: Baselines. Fit a single hyperplane, fundamentally underfitting temporal loops.
  • Support Vector Regression (SVR): Captures non-linearity using margins but scales poorly computationally (O(n2)).
  • Random Forest: Employs Bagging to aggregate hundreds of decision trees, capturing complex non-linear feature interactions (e.g., IF Hour=18 AND Day=Friday).
  • XGBoost: Sequential tree building utilizing built-in L1/L2 regularization.

7. Final Conclusions

1. Generalization vs Underfitting: Linear models failed entirely (~60% accuracy). The non-linear boundaries created by Decision Trees are essential for capturing cyclical traffic data.

2. Computational Complexity: Advanced models like SVR suffered from massive training latency (~36s), rendering them unviable for real-time scale.

3. Ultimate Winner: Highly optimized tree ensembles like Random Forest provided the ultimate balance, achieving a commanding 96.41% prediction accuracy in just 0.35 seconds.
